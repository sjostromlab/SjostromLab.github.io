How to use this simulation

Choose Mean & SDev to specify response statistics directly, or Binomial n, p, q to generate each response from the number of release sites (n), release probability (p), and quantal amplitude (q). The selected model applies to both A and B.

In binomial mode, a response is q times the number of successful releases, plus optional Gaussian background noise. Correct CV for background noise subtracts the known noise variance before calculating CV; uncheck it to see how recording noise can distort CV analysis. If the estimated release variance is zero or negative, the corrected CV is undefined.

One response is generated every 10 seconds, with a 5-minute induction gap. Reduce the response counts to see how limited sampling makes the observed result less reliable. New seed reruns the experiment. Uncheck Reuse seed to generate fresh responses whenever a parameter or response count changes.

The normalized coordinates are mean(after)/mean(before) and [CV(before)/CV(after)]². Theoretical markers use the entered population parameters; observed markers use sample statistics. A point's location does not uniquely identify a mechanism because the interpretation depends on the assumptions of the release model.

In binomial mode, changing p alters both mean response and release variability, whereas changing q scales the mean and SDev together and therefore leaves CV unchanged. This interpretation assumes uniform p and q, independent release sites, at most one quantum per site, and stable n. In real experiments, changing background noise, unstable baselines, measurement errors, and outliers can bias the result. CV analysis estimates the locus of plasticity and is best supported by independent measurements.
