How to explore
  1. Choose each lab laser at the same specimen power. Chirp efficiency reports only the dispersion penalty; relative 2p drive also responds to repetition rate, pulse duration and power.
  2. Switch to From components. Add estimated dispersion from the power-control optics, relay, objective, and other transmissive elements.
  3. Increase optical-path GDD while leaving average power unchanged. Pulse energy stays fixed, but peak power and chirp efficiency fall.
  4. Select Ideal compensation. It applies equal and opposite GDD, recovering the starting pulse in this idealized model.
  5. Compare the heating, per-pulse, and nonlinear-response cues for the 80 MHz and 25 MHz lasers.
Model, limitations & formulae

For a transform-limited Gaussian pulse with intensity FWHM τ₀ and net group-delay dispersion φ″, the delivered duration is τ = τ₀√[1 + (4 ln2 φ″/τ₀²)²]. Pulse energy is average power divided by repetition rate. Gaussian peak power is approximately 0.94 times pulse energy divided by pulse FWHM.

Chirp efficiency is τ₀/τ: the retained two-photon excitation relative to the same source without added dispersion. Relative 2p drive scales as P²/(fτ) and is normalized to an unchirped 20 mW, 80 MHz, 140 fs reference. It is a comparison index, not a prediction of fluorescence counts.

The component GDD values are user-adjustable teaching estimates, not a parts database. The model includes only second-order dispersion and omits focal area, third-order dispersion, spectral clipping, nonlinear propagation, wavelength-dependent transmission, fluorophore saturation, photodamage, and the detailed pulse shape of a real laser.
