What this simulation teaches

The galvos steer the focus in X and Y. The PMT supplies one brightness value at a time. The computer combines each value with the known beam position to build the image. More pixels improve sampling, not the optical resolution; longer dwell collects more photons but slows the frame.

Smaller pixels improve sampling, not optical resolution. Longer dwell increases expected photons per pixel, but slows acquisition. This ideal timing model does not guarantee that a real galvo can follow the requested waveform.

How to explore
  1. Move X and Y manually. Observe beam position before starting a raster.
  2. Change scan amplitude: field size and pixel spacing change together at fixed pixel count.
  3. Compare coarse and fine pixels. Check thin fibres and the close pair of bright points near the centre.
  4. Acquire a frame and follow the line-by-line scan. A single PMT signal is assigned to successive pixel positions.
  5. Enable shot noise and compare short with long dwell. The image gain stays fixed, while count uncertainty changes.
Model, formulas & references

A centred, correctly aligned paraxial relay with magnification M maps galvo optical angle θ to pupil angle −θ/M. Focal-plane position x = fθpupil. The full square field is 2fθmax/M. Pixel centres are spaced FOV/N apart; the first and last are half a pixel inside the field boundary. This page isolates image formation; return to the scanner page for separated-galvo back-aperture walk and clipping.

The synthetic specimen is a sum of Gaussian beads, soma-like objects, and straight Gaussian fibres. Optical blur is calculated analytically by convolution with a normalized Gaussian two-photon excitation PSF. The FWHM can be imported from the back-aperture page, but rings and axial structure of that page’s diffraction PSF are not transferred. The model uses point samples at pixel centres, without intra-pixel motion blur.

Physical frame time = N² × dwell / (1 − overhead fraction); overhead accounts collectively for flyback and other inactive time. The animation is deliberately slowed, and does not model real galvo acceleration, settling, bidirectional errors, or resonant scanning.

Optional photon counts follow a Poisson distribution with mean = local blurred specimen signal × bright-feature count rate × dwell. Count rate is an illustrative detected rate at fixed excitation and detection conditions, not a prediction from laser power. No background, electronic noise, bleaching, or photodamage is modelled. The half-FWHM pixel criterion is a teaching sampling heuristic, not an exact Nyquist criterion for every object or PSF.

Reading: Tsai et al., Principles, Design, and Construction of a Two-Photon Laser-Scanning Microscope; A pragmatic guide to multiphoton microscope design.
