Where are rays lost?

This is a geometric teaching model, not quantum efficiency. Filters, PMT spectral response, scattering and aberrations are excluded.

Alignment exercises

1 · Form a sharp back-aperture image. Adjust detector position until matrix coefficient B approaches zero. Rays leaving the same back-aperture point at different angles then meet at one detector point.

2 · Test stability. Sweep the mean ray angle. Correct conjugation keeps the image stationary even though individual rays take different paths.

3 · Fit the R3896. The image must fit within both 24 mm and 8 mm. The shorter dimension is normally limiting.

4 · Diagnose clipping. A correct image can still be dim if either collection lens is too small or badly placed.

One lens versus two lenses

The epi preset reflects the measured assembly: the 1-inch (25.4 mm) diameter, 25 mm focal-length lens is about 150 mm from the objective back aperture, and the PMT is about 20 mm beyond the lens at 170 mm. The substage preset uses the 50 mm LB1471-A field lens followed by a 25.4 mm focal-length focusing lens (LA1951-A green or LA1761-A red). The two-lens model treats either 25.4 mm focusing lens as ideal; their real surface forms and aberrations differ.

The 60 mm scan lens and 180 mm Olympus tube lens belong to the excitation scan relay. They image the galvos onto the objective back aperture and expand the excitation beam; they are not substituted into this detector model.

Imaging constraint
[ydet, θdet]T = M [yBA, θBA]T
M = [A B; C D]
Image plane: B = 0
Magnification: m = A

The ray-transfer matrix includes every propagation distance and thin lens. When B = 0, detector position no longer depends on input angle. For a chosen lens arrangement, the target detector distance is solved from B = 0.

Model limitations

Ideal paraxial thin lenses, centred elements and deterministic rays are used. Circular back aperture and lens clear apertures are combined with a rectangular detector. Diffraction, lens thickness, aberrations and detector housing are omitted. All lens positions refer to principal planes.
