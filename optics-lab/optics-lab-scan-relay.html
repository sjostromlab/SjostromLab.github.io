How to use this simulation
  1. Start with scanning off. Set 60 mm and 180 mm focal lengths. Adjust the lens separation toward 240 mm and compare beam diameters along the viewer-card plot.
  2. Switch to imaging. Scan X only, then Y only. Move the galvo block or objective objective back aperture to reduce footprint motion. A good telescope alone does not establish conjugation.
  3. Image X, then Y, then the midpoint. With separated mirrors you cannot make both footprints perfectly stationary. The midpoint balances the two ideal walk amplitudes.
  4. Use “Match 1/e² diameter to back aperture” to expose the tradeoff between aperture filling and clipping. Even a centred Gaussian has tails outside its 1/e² contour.
  5. Watch the specimen marker: the beam must change angle at the back aperture to move the focus. A stationary footprint does not mean an unchanging beam direction.
Model, limitations & references

Paraxial thin lenses in air, a circular Gaussian beam with M² = 1 and a flat wavefront at X, wavelength 920 nm by default. The complex Gaussian beam parameter is propagated by ABCD matrices. Lens apertures are unlimited. Two independent angular kicks occur at separated X and Y mirror planes; there is no relay between them.

The beam diameter uses the untruncated Gaussian solution. Power uses 8,192 deterministic Gaussian phase-space samples. A circular equivalent aperture at the block entrance represents its usable beam diameter; samples then propagate through both angular kicks to the objective back aperture. There is no second 3 mm clipping stop at Y. The physical Y mirror accommodates X-induced beam walk. This effective-aperture model assumes operation within the block’s supported scan range and near-collimated input; it cannot predict the real scan-dependent mirror-edge losses or transmission of Gaussian tails outside the rated beam envelope. Reported percentages are teaching estimates, not calibrated 6215H throughput. Real mirrors have projected, often noncircular clear apertures. This teaching approximation does not model edge diffraction, high-NA focusing, aberrations, pulse dispersion, or exact manufacturer mirror geometry. Power after clipping and an untruncated Gaussian envelope are deliberately distinct readouts.

“Approximately collimated” means less than 5% diameter variation over the displayed 50–1500 mm card range. Conjugation is assessed separately using B = 0 for each pivot plane; displayed motion uses the selected scan amplitude even when scanning is paused. A midpoint compromise is not perfect conjugation of either mirror.

Hardware documentation: Cambridge Technology 61021503R60, drawing D05613 Rev F, “6215 XY MOUNT, RH, Ø3 mm, 60° OPT,” supplied by the lab. The 0.229-inch scanner-axis separation gives 5.8166 mm, rounded to a 5.82 mm nominal pivot-spacing default. Using this as the optical path assumes reflections close to the rotation axes. Spacing remains adjustable. This mount drawing does not specify the reflecting outline of the installed Y mirror, so exact mirror-edge clipping remains unmodelled.

References: Tsai et al., Principles, Design, and Construction of a Two-Photon Laser-Scanning Microscope (2002), especially §6.3.5–6.3.7, including separated-galvo conjugation; Edmund Optics: Gaussian beam propagation; Coherent Chameleon Ultra specifications; Spectra-Physics Mai Tai specifications.
