From back-aperture illumination to focus

Explore how filling the objective back aperture changes focal confinement, power transmission and local two-photon excitation.

Illuminate the objective

Beam diameter is measured at 1/e² intensity. It is not a hard beam edge.

What stays constant?

Pulse duration, repetition rate and fluorophore properties stay fixed. The two-photon indicator describes a point-like fluorophore at the focus, not total fluorescence from a cell.

Objective & wavelength

Defaults are teaching settings, not a verified objective model. NA is constrained below the immersion refractive index. The equivalent focal length follows D/(2 NA), using the sine-condition pupil mapping.

How to explore
  1. Start with the back aperture underfilled, then increase the beam diameter. Watch lateral and axial confinement improve.
  2. Compare matched fill with overfilling. The focus approaches the uniformly illuminated back-aperture limit, while more incident power misses the aperture.
  3. Switch between constant power before and through the back aperture. Maintaining delivered power while overfilling requires more input power.
  4. Change wavelength or objective NA. Compare changes in lateral width and axial confinement.
Model, formulas & references

Scalar, rotationally symmetric, paraxial diffraction of a centred, unaberrated, truncated Gaussian illumination at the back aperture. With normalized back-aperture radius coordinate ρ and fill ratio t, field amplitude is exp(−ρ²/t²) for 0 ≤ ρ ≤ 1. Lateral amplitude is the integral over the illuminated back aperture times J₀(2π NA rρ/λ)ρ; axial amplitude includes exp(−iπ NA² zρ²/(nλ)). Intensities are squared field magnitudes; unsaturated two-photon excitation is proportional to I² at fixed pulse parameters. FWHM is calculated numerically, not from a hard cutoff at the nominal beam diameter.

Power transmission T = 1 − exp(−2/t²). The peak excitation comparison uses the coherent back-aperture integral with correct incident-power normalization; its reference is matched fill, 10 mW, with the same NA, wavelength and selected power mode. “Illumination-weighted NA” is NA × √(2⟨ρ²⟩), weighted by intensity across the back aperture. It is a descriptive measure, not a second objective specification or the formula used for FWHM.

This scalar paraxial model illustrates trends. It omits vector polarization effects at high NA, aberrations, refractive-index mismatch, scattering, saturation, bleaching, and aperture decentring. It is not an absolute fluorescence or high-NA PSF calibration.

Reading: Tsai et al., Principles, Design, and Construction of a Two-Photon Laser-Scanning Microscope, §6.3; A pragmatic guide to multiphoton microscope design; Standardised Measurements for Monitoring and Comparing Multiphoton Microscope Systems.
